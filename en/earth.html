Earth, day, and the seasons

Earth is the planet where we live. It has an atmosphere, a layer of gases surrounding the planet, and liquid water on its surface.

Earth spins on its axis. This motion is called rotation and takes about 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has daytime. The opposite side has nighttime.

Earth also moves around the Sun. One complete orbit takes about one year. This motion is called revolution.

Earth’s axis is tilted. This tilt changes how much light each hemisphere receives during the year and causes the seasons. The seasons are not mainly caused by the distance from the Sun.

Puno is in the Southern Hemisphere. Changes in rainfall and temperature also depend on a community’s location and altitude. To discuss local weather patterns, observe and consult records from the area.

An activity to share

Use a ball and a flashlight in a dark room. The ball represents Earth and the flashlight represents the Sun. Turn the ball and observe how its illuminated part changes.
