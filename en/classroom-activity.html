An activity for school

Starting question: why does the illuminated portion of the Moon that we see change? This activity uses a model. A model represents some features of a phenomenon but does not reproduce every detail.

Materials: a flashlight, a light-colored ball, and a space where the lights can be dimmed. The flashlight represents the Sun. The ball represents the Moon. The observer represents a viewpoint from Earth.

Keep the flashlight still. Another person slowly moves the ball around the observer. Hold the ball slightly above head level so the body does not block the light. Do not shine the flashlight into anyone’s eyes.

Observe which part of the ball receives light and how much of that illuminated part you can see. Draw three positions. Discuss the difference between the illuminated part and the visible part.

The model does not represent real distances or sizes. It also cannot explain eclipse dates by itself. Its purpose is to show how our view of an illuminated sphere changes.

An activity to share

Each participant explains one drawing in their preferred language. Record any remaining questions to investigate in another session.
