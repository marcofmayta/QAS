The Moon and its phases

The Moon is Earth’s natural satellite. A natural satellite is a body that travels around a planet. The Moon reflects sunlight.

Half of the Moon receives sunlight. From Earth, we see different portions of that illuminated half. This makes the Moon appear to change shape. These changes are called phases.

At new moon, the illuminated side faces mainly away from Earth. At full moon, we see almost all of that illuminated side. Waxing and waning phases occur between them. The cycle lasts about 29 and a half days.

Earth’s shadow does not cause the phases. Earth’s shadow can darken the Moon during a lunar eclipse. The Moon can also be visible during the day, depending on its phase and position.

An activity to share

Observe the Moon for a week whenever it is visible. Draw its illuminated part and record the date and time. If you cannot see it, note whether clouds or buildings blocked that direction.
